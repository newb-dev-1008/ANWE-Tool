# Tierra del Fuego: ethical basis and human–animal trade-offs

Tierra del Fuego adopts animal-focused ethics. Sentient animals have welfare interests that matter independently of human benefits. Restoration decisions can affect those interests, so people making those decisions must consider avoidable harm and justify trade-offs. Human contributions to habitat damage can strengthen responsibilities, but this project does not assume a human ignition cause for the Culebra fire.

This is a normative position adopted by the project. Satellite indices, occurrence records and AI output cannot prove the position or establish that an intervention improves welfare.

## Why a modest animal-welfare priority?

The project focuses on animal welfare, and affected animals cannot represent themselves in ordinary land-use decisions. These considerations justify deliberately giving their welfare interests additional attention rather than treating unrepresented interests as negligible. The project focus explains the design emphasis; the independent moral relevance of sentient animals supplies the ethical reason.

The priority is modest and defeasible. When feasible options remain closely balanced after considering credible evidence, severity, likelihood, duration, reversibility and affected interests, favour the option with lower credible risk of animal harm. This is a reasoned preference, not a numerical weight, legal authority or automatic pathway recommendation.

Human safety, essential livelihoods and other serious needs require explicit consideration. A credible urgent threat may justify proportionate intervention with mitigation and monitoring. Commercial convenience alone does not justify ignoring credible severe avoidable animal harm. Human interests are not treated as morally irrelevant, and ecological benefits do not automatically cancel individual animal harms.

Uncertainty warrants proportionate caution, surveys, reversible measures or deferral where safe. It does not prove occupancy, sentience or suffering, and it must not obstruct necessary urgent protective action. Evaluate harms of inaction as well as harms of intervention. Neither natural regeneration nor active restoration is inherently preferred.

## Decision record

For each pathway, identify:

1. The human objective and who benefits or bears costs.
2. Affected animal interests and the evidence for them.
3. The possible conflict, including ecological objectives.
4. Less harmful feasible alternatives and the consequences of delay.
5. Missing evidence, affected groups and proportional field checks.
6. Safeguards, monitoring and stopping criteria.
7. The human justification, including necessity and proportionality if animal interests are outweighed.

The comparison contains authored prompts for natural regeneration, assisted restoration and active restoration. They are potential conflicts to investigate, not verified claims about local harm. Field evidence and qualified review remain necessary. No aggregate ethical score is produced and no intervention is authorised.

## Implementation and limits

The canonical policy and pathway prompts are in `data/design-values.json`, mirrored in the browser's generated `data/design-values.js`. The Design for Values screen explains the ethical basis and its limits. Each pathway assessment displays a structured human–animal comparison. The human review form requests a reasoned justification and permits deferral. The existing rationale field stores this explanation, with existing private-export consent unchanged. The app does not automatically determine whether a justification is morally sufficient.

The AI receives the same policy and authored prompts. This does not guarantee a model will follow them or reason correctly. It must not invent site-specific welfare facts or select an approved winner. Reports explain the modest priority and retain full policy and trade-off prompts in Appendix C. Older exported reports and AI turns are historical artifacts and are not retroactively revised or treated as generated under the new policy.

Design for Values operationalises this ethical commitment through norms, requirements, features and evaluation. It does not replace the underlying moral argument. Ecological recovery remains a substantive objective; transparency, uncertainty awareness and accountability govern the decision process.

## Ethical background

- [Clare Palmer (2010), Animal Ethics in Context](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/animal-ethics-in-context/9780231503020/): context and human relationships can affect responsibilities towards wild animals.
- [Dale Jamieson (1998), Animal Liberation is an Environmental Ethic](https://doi.org/10.3197/096327198129341465): an animal-focused ethic can support strong environmental commitments.
- [Capozzelli, Hecht and Halsey (2020), What is the value of wild animal welfare for restoration ecology?](https://doi.org/10.1111/rec.13114): ecosystem processes and individual welfare can align or conflict.

These sources inform the project. They do not establish a universally accepted priority ordering or validate this prototype's ecological outcomes.
